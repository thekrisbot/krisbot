Scratch That

A sketch on sketches. Ooooh we get it, it's meta.
August 11, 2026

SETTING: A SCRATCH NIGHT. SMALL ROOM, PACKED AUDIENCE.

HOST. 30s. Warm, and spontaneous. Plays a straight man.

SIMON. Judge. Blunt, elitist, snobbish. 

ASHLEY. Judge. Sweet but deadly.

MARTIN. Judge. Fad-merchant.

WRITER. Cameo.

(Lights up. HOST stands centre stage, applauding.)

HOST

Give it up one more time for the cast of Scratch That. It was so raw, emotional, and uplifting! And seeing the reaction in the room, I think it's safe to say we all loved it?

(Real audience applauds.)

So, part of the fun of a scratch night is the feedback. So the three of you who signed up on the way in, come on up, you're tonight's judges! Wohooo!!

(SIMON, ASHLEY, and MARTIN make their way to a small table with three chairs, already set up like a panel.)

Now, let's meet tonight's panel. We've got Simon.

(SIMON gives a small, unimpressed nod, like he's doing everyone a favour.)

We've got the beautiful Ashley.

(ASHLEY beams and gives a little wave, utterly delighted to be there.)

And of course, Martin.

(MARTIN gives a thumbs up, then seems to reconsider it halfway for something cooler.)

(gleefully)

So, judges, what did we think of the piece? Simon?

(Beat. SIMON leans into the imaginary mic.)

SIMON

Yeah, look, it wasn't bad. Pacing really dragged in the middle, but hey if we wanted anything better, we’d have to go to Edinburgh, right?

(Impressed at himself, nobody else in the room laughs.) 


HOST

Oooh, let’s take it easy Simon, I thought they did a phenomenal job considering it's a 5-minute—

(She starts to turn to MARTIN. SIMON is not finished.)

SIMON

Phen? Are you kidd.. I've seen better writing on the walls of a Wetherspoon toilet than that one!

HOST

Woah.

SIMON

And I say that as someone who wants it to work. Maybe if they had a dramaturg it could have been salvaged..

HOST

A dramaturg?

SIMON

Yeah, someone whose entire job is to sit there and tell you why it isn't working. Which, frankly, I'm doing for free right now.

HOST

And you've, done this before?

SIMON

Yeah, yeah, I've taken a free Masterclass course on drama that came with my Lime subscription. It’s all the same.

HOST

I.. frankly don't know how to react to that. Umm, Martin?

MARTIN

Yeah, I just didn't feel like it was speaking to anyone under thirty and it missed a major ingredient.

HOST

Oh what’s that? 

MARTIN

Pokémon.

HOST

I'm sorry, Pokémon, as in?

MARTIN

See, as a writer, you've got to always keep an eye out, feel that pulse, and see what is dominating the conversation in the public square. And Pokemon?

(clicks his fingers multiple times)

MARTIN

That's all the rage, baby.

HOST

Well, to be fair, the sketch was a spin on The Odyssey, set in the 8th century B.C, so I am not sure how you fit in a..

MARTIN

(cutting in, entirely unbothered)

Doesn't matter. You've got to give the TikTok generation something to hold onto, or you lose them in the first ten seconds.

(HOST realising she’s losing control)

HOST

Okay, well, perhaps what would serve well is, if we start off with what we liked. Ashley?

(ASHLEY clears her throat, beaming.)

ASHLEY

Oh, bless them. I don't think I've seen anything so brave

HOST

Aw, that's really sweet of you, Ashley.

ASHLEY

(cutting in, still beaming)

to take a piece that is so clearly not ready, and present it in a room. In front of a panel too?

HOST

Oh.

(Beat. She recovers, turns to the audience.)

HOST

(breaking the fourth wall)

Just a reminder, for anyone new, scratch nights are for work in progress pieces, so we do try not to be too critical. If you don't have something to say that's fine don't feel compelled to—

ASHLEY

Ooh, and I wasn't the biggest fan of that thing right under his nose, poor lamb.

HOST

Right under his..You mean his lips?

ASHLEY

Mm-hmm I'd put some Ozempic on it, if I were him.

(looking into her makeup mirror, fixing her lips)

(MARTIN claps and points at ASHLEY.)

MARTIN

Ozempic. Brilliant. Another in-the-now element. See, we are already making the piece so much better. Hope the writer is noting it all down.

HOST

Oh, I am sure they are. But is there anything you actually liked? 

ASHLEY

Ooh, now that you mention it, yes.

HOST

Oh thank god,

ASHLEY

The rhinestones. They really worked for me.

HOST

The rhine—


ASHLEY

Yes, on her heels, the tall one. I have been looking for a pair myself, so if you'd be a dear, please, and let me know where I can find that..

(HOST closes her eyes for a beat, gathering herself.)

HOST

What is happening here!?

(Behind them, unnoticed, WRITER rises quietly from the audience and begins making their way to the front.)

MARTIN

(mid-thought, half to himself)

Honey, let me sum it up like how you would understand. It's like, not even ********** could have saved this one.

(A beat. From the back of the room, WRITER's voice, calm and amused.)

WRITER

(from the audience, in the back)

Actually, I wrote it.

(A beat. WRITER makes their way to the front. Unhurried. Entirely at ease.)

WRITER

It's fine. Honestly. Everyone in this room has notes, and most of them mean well. 

(Beat. Comes further forward.)

But I just want to say, everything you have ever loved started like this. Somebody wrote it, nobody had seen it, and they stood in a room about this size to find out whether it worked. That is all a scratch night is. It isn't the show. It's the bit before the show, where you get to be wrong in front of people, which is the only way anyone ever gets any good.

(Beat. A sip of water.)

So everything you see tonight is unfinished. On purpose. Some of it will land. Some of it won't. But all I ask is, be kind to the folks who are giving their best.

(Beat.)

Laugh if it's funny, cry if it is sad. 

And if it isn't working for you, that’s fine. 

But just remember that somebody wrote that at two in the morning, absolutely convinced it will.

(Beat.)

That said, we have a wonderful evening, ladies and gentlemen!

(Blackout.)
