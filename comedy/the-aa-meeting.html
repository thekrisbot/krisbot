THE AA MEETING

Hello my name is _______ and I have a_______
October 15, 2026

CHARACTERS:

GARETH. 40s. Founder and facilitator. Motivating.

DEBS. 40s. Burglar. Level-headed.

KEVIN. 20s. Burglar. Free-spirited kid.

BRIAN. 60s. Shoplifter. Grumpy.

NASIR. 30s. Car thief. Nonchalant.


SETTING: A community hall somewhere in London. Five plastic chairs in a circle. A banner reading “ANARCHISTS ANONYMOUS: BECOMING BETTER.” Beneath it, a laminated sign: “Free Biscuits. Please do not take the tin.”

(Lights up. Four people sit in the circle. GARETH stands, a mug of tea in one hand and his phone in the other, thumbs still moving. He looks up, beams, and slides the phone into his pocket.)


GARETH

Hello everybody! Welcome to Anarchists Anonymous. I believe this is the first time this particular little group is convening. So I just want to quickly set the context. My name is Gareth, I am the Founder and Facilitator here. I’ve been running AA for the last 4 months. This space was born out of a real need for our community members with a few quirks... This is a safe space. Everything we say here is confidential. We don’t judge, we don’t interrupt. That said, do we have any volunteers to go first?

(Debs raises her hand.)

DEBS

Hi! My name is Debbie.

EVERYONE

Hello, Debs.

DEBS

I have been on the job for twelve years now.

(Everyone claps and encourages.)

DEBS

I have done hundreds of burglaries. Flats, Victorian houses, you name it. But I am a sucker for jigsaw puzzles... If I see an unfinished jigsaw when I am robbing a house, I am a lost cause.

(Everyone murmurs.)

DEBS

It’s been two weeks since I’ve relapsed, which is my personal best, the last streak was twenty five days in 2024. I’ve been arrested at least 20 times in the last 5 years because of this.

EVERYONE

Wow.

DEBS

Here’s what happens. I go in with a plan, jewellery, the gadgets, sometimes even the telly, if I feel like it. Anyway, it takes me maybe 30 minutes to wrap up but god forbid I bump into a jigsaw in a guest room or something, that’s it. The plan goes for a toss and I am at their dining table with the study lamp on sorting out the right corner of a Prairie.

(Everyone reacts silently.)

DEBS

Last October, I was in a house in Kensington. Half past two in the morning, and I have easily cleared 200 grand. Easily. The watch alone, a custom one, the bloke was too rich to even put it in the safe. Then I notice this 4000-piece Venice landscape that is half-way done. Just lying there on the floor in one of the upstairs bedrooms. And I swear it only seemed like a few minutes and I am down to the last 200 pieces maybe and next thing I know, this bright yellowish light hits me right on my face.

BRIAN

What was it?

DEBS

The bloody sun had come up. The birds chirping and everything.

NASIR

And?

DEBS

What else? Before I could blink I was getting handcuffed and taken down to the police station.

GARETH

Oh that’s rough sweetie.

(Debs breaks down.)

GARETH

It’s alright, let it out. That’s what we are all here for.

DEBS

You know the worst part? That I haven’t admitted to anyone. I care more about the 200 pieces left on that puzzle than the 200,000 I was about to make.

(Everyone silently takes it in, murmuring.)

KEVIN

Ummm, have you tried not doing a jigsaw puzzle in the middle of a burglary.

(Everyone rolls their eyes as Kevin sheepishly apologises.)

DEBS

(sarcastically)

Oh, thank you so much darling. Why didn’t I think of that?

KEVIN

Sorry.

BRIAN

Here’s a thought. When you look at a puzzle, you probably feel like you have to get to it right then because you don’t know if you will see it again. How about just taking it along with the other things you are stealing?

EVERYONE

Yeah, that’s a great idea.

DEBS

Well, actually, I did try that a couple times but what happened was there were always a few pieces that go missing. Usually stuck under the couch or in a random corner of the house. Especially if they have a pet or a child... And I can’t go back obviously and it drove me even crazier...

GARETH

Thank you so much for sharing that Debs, we will get back to you. Sir, do you want to go next?

NASIR

Yeah, yeah. That’s fine. Hello, everyone. I’m Nasir.

EVERYONE

Hello, Nasir.

NASIR

I am a Car Pro. I can hotwire a luxury sedan faster than this lad can scoff a biscuit... but...

GARETH

It’s okay Nasir, take your time...

(EVERYONE nodding in approval as NASIR takes a sip.)

NASIR

But I can’t drive away just like that without...

KEVIN

Without?

NASIR

Without making sure that my seat is set up proper. Sometimes I spend more than half-an-hour ensuring that the ergonomics are perfect and as you probably guessed, it has got me in trouble now and then...

DEBS

I’m sorry, ergonomics... Like the seat?

(NASIR shamefully nods.)

KEVIN

Wait, you mean you sit there in a bloody stolen vehicle and don’t drive away until your bum is perfectly lined up against them seat warmers?? I thought I had it bad...

(KEVIN chortles mockingly then apologises.)

NASIR

Hey! I’ve got a bad back, alright?

GARETH

Let’s take it easy guys. Kevin, just a reminder... We are all here because we have quirks that those outside this room cannot empathise with, right? So let’s try to be more supportive.

KEVIN

(hands up in the air)

Yeah, yeah. Sorry mate.

NASIR

Have you seen the way some people leave their seats? The last bloke must have been five foot nothing. My knees were jammed

right up into my chin. I have terrible sciatica. Took me fifteen minutes to sort it out...

DEBS

Oh, the sciatica is hell!

BRIAN

Fifteen minutes... I remember back in my day car seats had two options... Flat down or straight up... none of this button nonsense...

(Everyone chuckles.)

NASIR

Tell me about it! This April I was in one of those electric cars and after wrestling with the seat for a good ten minutes I finally had it locked down only to realise that I had to ‘save my preferences’. It literally reverted back to the owner’s settings. So I opened the glovebox and started sifting through the car manual to find out how to lock my settings as a ‘preset’.

KEVIN

Well, did you get it?

NASIR

Yeah, I got it alright. Six months at the Scrubs. Turns out while I was busy playing around with the seat settings the owner was standing outside with a policeman, watching the whole thing. Then he taps on the glass and asks if I need a hand finding the button.

(The group lets out stifled laughter.)

DEBS

(slightly drunk)

Oh, my Ron needed a map and torch for that... And managed to find the button twice in thirty years of marriage...

(puts the drink down)

DEBS (CONT’D)

Oops.

KEVIN

Hey mate, one of my flatmates from uni is actually a physio... I’ve just shared his contact on the WhatsApp group...

DEBS

Roommate? How did that happen?

KEVIN

Oh, I dropped out of med school a few years ago...

NASIR

What? Where did you go?

KEVIN

UCL...

NASIR

(mouthing to himself)

Wow.

DEBS

Sweetie, what are you doing with this rag-tag no good bunch?

KEVIN

Why does everyone keep asking me that? It’s not that crazy... Elizabeth Holmes, SBF, Bernie Madoff all went to fancy universities before deciding to swindle millions...

NASIR

Yeah, the kid’s got a point... Didn’t that Ted Bundy go to Harvard as well before he started you know...

BRIAN

No, you are thinking of Ted Kaczynski... The unabomber...

GARETH

Actually, if I remember right, Ted Bundy went to University of Washington, which is top university as well.

DEBS

Huh. Fair enough, point taken!

GARETH

Well that was a fun little detour. Brian, would you like to...

BRIAN

If I have to... I am Brian...

EVERYONE

Hello, Brian.

BRIAN

I am a shoplifter... I started out during the dot com crash... I was living in America at that time... I had just lost my job and insurance... my wife was seven months pregnant with our son and we couldn’t even afford the meds...

(BRIAN looks into the distance, lost in thought.)

BRIAN (CONT’D)

This is so embarrassing... what am I doing here here... you know what... maybe this was not a good idea...

(BRIAN gets up, ready to leave as the group tries to persuade.)

GARETH

Brian, Brian. Please. You have already taken the brave step coming down here. And none of us are here to collect our best lawbreaker awards. We are all flawed, as you can clearly see... Whatever it is... This little step... you opening up today... that could help some pickpocket teen in Peckham stay out of prison tomorrow. Please?

(GARETH takes BRIAN’s arm.)

BRIAN

Alright fine, if Sciatica Joe here can make a fool of himself so can I...

EVERYONE

Yes! Way to go Brian!

(NASIR mumbles “cheap shot”.)

BRIAN

My issue is... When I am nicking things in the shop, I end up helping out other customers on the floor...

NASIR

What do you mean?

KEVIN

Like, you steal stuff for others?

BRIAN

What? No... what am I running a charity shop here??

DEBS

Then?

BRIAN

You know when somebody is on the hygiene aisle and they are looking for a soda... and there is no bloody floor assistant ever these days... So I show them around and help pick out what they want...

DEBS

Aww, that is so adorable...

KEVIN

We’ve got Batman Brian! Woo!!

BRIAN

Yeah, I knew it... Let’s all laugh at the old clown who can’t swipe a steak without picking out the meal deal options for the lady with the stroller...

DEBS

No... I am sure nobody thinks that... It’s really sweet, I am being honest... Right guys?

(EVERYONE mumbles in unison.)

BRIAN

I don’t know... it’s just... I feel like I am seeing fewer and fewer shop assistants... And it just makes me so angry... the corporations making more and more money... while the innocent customers out there looking like a knobhead... Wandering these big stores with no help... hoping if someone would make it a little easy for them...

(EVERYONE acknowledges quietly.)

BRIAN (CONT’D)

Last week, I am in one of the supermarkets... The one with the flower in its name...

(The group chimes in with the name.)

BRIAN (CONT’D)

And this poor sod... In his 70s... Doesn’t understand why his meal deal came up to £8.95 and he is looking around so confused... he was ready to break down right there I swear... And not one muppet shows up... You want me to just look the other way... my mother didn’t raise me a pig...

GARETH

Brian, thank you so much for sharing that experience. I get it... With cost-cutting, AI, Farage... customer service has gone for a toss...

KEVIN

I guess that just leaves me... Hi, everyone. I’m Kevin.

EVERYONE

Hello, Kevin.

KEVIN

Anyway, I break into houses... Same as Debs... but I haven’t been at this for long... maybe we can exchange notes or something...

I have a problem with laundry... whenever I am on the job, if I notice dirty clothes lying around, I can’t help but put it in the machine and go about business while they get washed...

NASIR

Boy, we really saved the best for last eh, walking launderette?

(GARETH shoots a look and NASIR gestures an apology.)

KEVIN

I didn’t realise it for a while because I kept getting away with it somehow... sometimes no one’s home... sometimes they are busy with their headphones on... This one time in Stratford the bloke assumed that a flatmate had turned on the washing machine... but it was only a matter of time I guess...

My last arrest was at a student hall in Victoria. Found this huge pile on one of those bamboo laundry baskets. Swear, taller than you Gareth. I couldn’t resist. I load the machine, pour the detergent, and in a few minutes someone at the top of the stairs says “who’s put a wash on?” Turned out nobody had touched the machine in two months.

DEBS

Kevin, what is it about dirty clothes that makes you want to get a handle on it right away?

BRIAN

Yeah, isn’t it fashionable for you kids these days to dress like homeless bums anyway?

KEVIN

(chuckling lightly)

I’ve never admitted this to anyone, but I think it has to do with my mum. She ran a tight ship. One dirty pair of socks lying around, and she’d lose it. My sister and I grew up walking on eggshells. We’d have the cleanest uniforms in all of primary school.

GARETH

Oh, that is lovely...

KEVIN

I guess when my mum passed away couple years back, I kind of lost my way... And got tossed into a different world... and this laundry thing became a weird way of staying in touch with her spirit.

(Kevin wipes away a tear.)

KEVIN (CONT’D)

I am sorry, I am just rambling...

EVERYONE

Oh Kevin, awww.

(The group round him up to give a hug.)

(Gareth’s phone buzzes. He looks down, delighted, and taps out a like haphazardly.)

GARETH

That was really beautiful Kevin, thank you. I know how frustrating it can be to do what we do with these quirks... but in our line of work, it is very easy to get carried away and forget the little details that make us, us. A loving son, a caring stranger... And we must hold onto them... because without it we are just another looter with no moral compass...

(As GARETH is about to finish, two police personnel walk in.)

POLICE OFFICER

We are from the Met.

(to the other officer.)

POLICE OFFICER (CONT’D)

Get the cuffs.

NASIR

(hands behind)

What? How? Gareth, you rat. Did you set us up??

BRIAN

I knew it!!

GARETH

God no!!

(The officers drag away the rest of the group.)

GARETH (CONT’D)

My name is Gareth. I am a social media addict. I live tweet every one of these meetings and the Met promptly show up right after. They don’t even bother locking me up because I am more valuable for them on the outside than behind bars.

I am sorry, I am so sorry!!

(GARETH breaks down, kneeling and screaming on the floor.)

(Blackout.)
