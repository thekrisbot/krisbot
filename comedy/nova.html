A · Comedy

Nova

A Halloween night and a cold case.
August 14, 2026

CHARACTERS:

DETECTIVE CONSTABLE RONNIE SISK. Retired. A rookie in the flashback.

ELLIS CROOM. In his thirties. Civilian.

DETECTIVE INSPECTOR FOZ PEREZ. Forties. Experienced, no-nonsense investigative officer.

TANYA. Sixteen. Ronnie's granddaughter.

TOBES. Fourteen. Ronnie's grandson.

NOVA. The missing woman. Does not appear in the play.

SETTING: A LIVING ROOM, THEN A FLASHBACK

(RONNIE, older now, with a plate on his knee. TANYA and TOBES have their coats on.)

TANYA

Grandpa, you said you had one. You have to tell us!

RONNIE

I said I had one. I never said I'd tell it.

TOBES

It's Halloween!! It's tradition to tell spooky stories.

RONNIE

Alright, alright.

(RONNIE puts the plate down.)

RONNIE (CONT'D)

Thirty-one years I was in the force. Seen all kinds of gore. Bodies out the canal, a fella in Bromley who kept his own mother in the freezer for two years and still sent her Christmas cards out in her own handwriting, but none made a mark like this one.

Barely a year into my job, a bloke walked in to report a woman missing.

(He stands. The kids sit at the table and slowly fade out for the flashback.)

INTERVIEW ROOM. NOVEMBER 2005.

(RONNIE with a clipboard. ELLIS with a hardback notebook in front of him, closed, both hands resting on it.)

RONNIE

Right, before we start I've got to read you back what we took Friday and you tell me if any of it's wrong.

Reported missing, Friday the fourth of November, 2005. Reported by Ellis Croom. Name of missing person, Nova. Surname, blank. Date of birth, blank. Age, twenty-seven to thirty, question mark, because you told me that was a guess.

Home address, blank. Place of work, blank. Next of kin, blank. GP, blank.

(He turns the sheet over. Nothing on the back. He turns it back.)

ELLIS

Did you try the phone number? Did somebody pick up?

RONNIE

No, Mr Croom. The digits you gave us were one short. It's not a valid phone number.

ELLIS

Well what does that mean?

RONNIE

It means either you've given us a wrong one, or⁠—

ELLIS

That's, that's the one she gave me. That's the one she wrote down for me, I swear to you.

(RONNIE puts the clipboard down and rubs his eyes.)

RONNIE

Ellis, I'll be honest with you. Looking at that form, that's not a lot to go on. I can't knock on a door with it, I can't put her on a system, I can't ring anybody.

So give me something. It doesn't have to be big. A pub she liked. The road she lived on. You haven't even given me the name of a friend.

(ELLIS thinks about it properly. He wants to help.)

ELLIS

I don't know, all right? I've told you everything I've got.

The bus... the bus.. She got the 453 sometimes, and then half the time she'd say she was getting the 453 and she'd walk instead, because the movement did her head in and she'd rather be cold.

She liked the chip shop by the roundabout, the one with the wonky sign.

(Beat. RONNIE waits for more. There isn't more.)

RONNIE

Alright, the 453. Did she say where she got off?

ELLIS

No. No. But it might be in here, that's the thing, so if there's something you need I'll have got it, you just tell me what you're after and I'll find it for you.

RONNIE

Can I take a look at that, if you don't mind?

(ELLIS slides it across without letting go, then lets go.)

(RONNIE opens it. Reads. Turns a page. Turns another.)

RONNIE

"Fifth October. Sixteen twelve to eighteen forty. Grey shoes, scuffed on the left toe, drags that foot when she's tired. Half a Twix, puts the rest in her coat pocket for later, never eats it."

ELLIS

She never did eat it. I'd find them in the coat weeks after, all soft..

RONNIE

"Sixth October. Sixteen thirty-seven. She was late. That damned manager of hers. I like her blue dress."

(RONNIE turns pages faster now, looking for something.)

RONNIE

"Third November. Still no sign. Did I hurt her somehow?"

(Silence. RONNIE closes the notebook and stands.)

RONNIE

Give me two minutes.

ELLIS

Where are you going?

RONNIE

Don't worry about it.

(He goes, taking the notebook.)

(RONNIE returns with FOZ, who is reading the notebook as she walks and doesn't stop reading when she sits. RONNIE stays standing.)

FOZ

Mr Croom. Detective Inspector Foz Perez. I've had this for about five minutes, so forgive me if I go over ground you've already done.

ELLIS

No, anything that helps find my Nova!

FOZ

Tell me about your time with Nova, Mr Croom.

(ELLIS puts a hand out for the notebook. FOZ doesn't give it to him. He carries on anyway.)

ELLIS

I met her at Regent's. She was stood by the boating lake having a go at the crowds, this whole thing about how nobody in this city knows how to walk, and I'd been having exactly the same thought about four seconds before she said it out loud.

She smelled of plastic. That's the first thing I wrote down about her that day.

FOZ

Did she tell you what she was doing there?

ELLIS

No, no. Maybe she was meeting somebody.

FOZ

Who did she meet?

ELLIS

I don't know, I don't know.. I didn't see no one with her while I was there..

FOZ

And after that, did you meet her daily?

(FOZ leafing through the notebook.)

ELLIS

Yes, yes. Maybe the one weekend, when she had to go away on a trip.. We didn't meet then.

FOZ

Would you say she was your girlfriend, Ellis?

Can I, can I call you Ellis?

(ELLIS nods.)

ELLIS

I don't know about that, she did always say we will see about it. But we did spend a lot of time together.

FOZ

And where would that be?

ELLIS

Southbank.. She didn't mind the chilly weather, said she was used to it.

FOZ

What does that mean?

ELLIS

I don't know..

FOZ

And you didn't ask her?

(ELLIS shakes his head, facing down at the table.)

FOZ

What else did you do?

ELLIS

We.. we would stay in a lot... cook meals.. She loved my pasta.

FOZ

Oh. Where was this?

ELLIS

My flat.. It was always my flat. She wouldn't have me at hers, there were flatmates and she said they were horrendous, and I said horrendous how, and she did this thing where she'd change the subject without changing the subject.

And I let her, because you do. Three weeks in you think, right, that's a bit she doesn't want to do yet.

(FOZ turns a page. Doesn't look up.)

FOZ

Where does she work?

ELLIS

Retail. That's all I ever got.

FOZ

Just "retail"?

(FOZ closes the notebook and puts her hand flat on it.)

FOZ

Let's forget about the girl for a second, Ellis. Tell me about you.

ELLIS

I am thirty two. I live in Lambeth.

FOZ

Constable Sisk tells me that you have a condition.

(ELLIS does not respond, lost in his own thought.)

FOZ

Ellis?

(ELLIS looks up slowly.)

ELLIS

The Alzheimer's? Found out a few months back.

FOZ

I'm sorry...

ELLIS

Everyone says that. It's fine.

FOZ

What do you want them to tell?

ELLIS

What?

FOZ

What do you want them to say when you talk about Alzheimer's?

ELLIS

Nothing. I was not one for conversation much.. At least until she...

(ELLIS drifts off, never finishing the thought.)

FOZ

Tell me about Nova, Ellis.

ELLIS

Well, Nova was special. She was beautiful, funny, and I never understood... why she would ever spend any of her life with a guy like me.

(ELLIS tearing up, looking afar as the room falls silent. FOZ picks up the book again.)

FOZ

Now, the notebook. Tell me about it.

ELLIS

Well, I use it to keep track of things..

FOZ

What things?

ELLIS

Everything.. Details.. Names.. Phone numbers.. Directions to my house..

(Restrained sniffles.)

FOZ

Right. Then do you mind if I ask you a follow-up, Ellis?

(ELLIS nods slowly.)

FOZ

You say the notebook has everything.. Yet every entry I see is that of a woman you'd known for a few weeks.

ELLIS

I had?

FOZ

(clears throat)

I am sorry. A woman you have known for a few weeks.

ELLIS

We already went this over.

This one's all Nova because I've got the other ones at home. Big ones, the whole shelf.. I've been doing it since they diagnosed me in March.

This one I carry, because I wanted to be able to help. And this one's all her. What she liked, how her day went, what her hair was doing..

(FOZ leafs through the notebook.)

FOZ

You know, it's interesting you said that.. Because I am going through the entries you've made and they don't make sense. In this page, her hair's dark.. Few pages later she's a blonde, and she's magically dark again...

(Pause.)

ELLIS

Because she had these hairpieces. Put em' on her head all the time.. Not every day, now and again, and half the time she'd forgotten she had one on and I'd have to tell her, and she'd get the giggles about it.

That was a whole running thing with us. It was just one of the many jokes we had. Just us.

(FOZ turns to a page.)

FOZ

"Twenty-second October. The blood is not real. She is not hurting. She is fine. Don't ask her again, she gets mad."

(She looks up with intrigue.)

FOZ

Talk to me about that.

ELLIS

She'd come round with it in her hands. Up her sleeves sometimes. She wouldn't have clocked it till I said something.

I did ask her, the first time. She held her hands up and she went, looks real, doesn't it. And then I smelled, they were actually not real. She just washed it off giggling.

FOZ

Ellis, a woman you have barely known comes into your home with a bloody hand and you were not the least bit curious?

ELLIS

Of course I was!

FOZ

Then?

ELLIS

She just said another day at work and if I asked any further she'd change the subject.

FOZ

And you didn't think it was weird?

ELLIS

Of course I did.

FOZ

Well, it didn't bother you?

ELLIS

No... because it didn't look real. It looked like blood they use in a film.

(Beat. RONNIE writes that down. ELLIS watches him write it down.)

ELLIS

What's he written?

(RONNIE looks at FOZ, as she ignores his question.)

FOZ

Ellis, I'm going to be straight with you, from what I have heard so far, I can't look for her. Not can't be bothered. We just can't.

You come in with a missing complaint yet there is no last name, no photograph, no employer details, no landlords, no parents, no friends.

If I put this out tomorrow the appeal says woman, twenties, possibly dark hair.

So the way it works from here is we start with the last person who saw her and work from there. In this case that happens to be...

ELLIS

Me.

(FOZ nods.)

FOZ

Tell me about the last day you saw her. Thirty-first of October.

ELLIS

She didn't come. Half four went, five went, and I sat there like a lemon until about nine and then I told myself she'd been held up.

First of November I went and waited again by the park. All day, near enough, because I thought if she's lost my address that's the one place she knows to find me.

FOZ

All day?

ELLIS

Till it got dark. Second of November I cleaned the flat.

(FOZ's pen stops as she looks up.)

ELLIS

What?

FOZ

Nothing.

ELLIS

I clean when things go wrong. My mother was the same, she'd have the kitchen out at two in the morning. I did the oven. I was in there eleven hours, because if I stopped I had to sit down, and if I sat down I had to think about it.

FOZ

And on the fourth you came to us.

ELLIS

Yes.

FOZ

Who else have you told?

(Beat.)

ELLIS

What do you mean?

FOZ

A week she's been gone. Who else knows?

(ELLIS opens his mouth and nothing comes.)

FOZ

Anybody. A mate of yours, a neighbour, your brother, the man in the shop.

ELLIS

...No. Nobody.

RONNIE

Ma'am⁠—

(FOZ puts her hand up as RONNIE grows quiet.)

FOZ

A woman allegedly goes missing but nobody has reported her missing except you. Everything in the world that says this person existed is under my hand. In this notebook.

ELLIS

Then use it to find her!

FOZ

With what? Give me a road, a shop, a birthday, a surname. Anything I can type in.

(Nothing.)

ELLIS

I know it's not enough.

FOZ

You didn't ask her surname. You didn't ask where she lived. You didn't ask what the hairpieces were for.

She came to your flat with blood up her sleeve and told you it was another day at work, and you shrugged, and you moved on to something else. Yet, there are pages and pages of entries about how she smelled and her favourite candy bar?

(ELLIS grows agitated, as RONNIE notices.)

RONNIE

Ma'am, maybe we should take a fi⁠—

FOZ

Ronnie, sit down.

ELLIS

(shouting)

That's not fair!

FOZ

It's not that the notebook is not enough. It's how perfectly curated it is. Twenty-two years I've been doing this and I've never had a person vanish and leave nothing. You've brought me a woman with none of it and a book that says she was in your flat every day for six weeks, timed, dated, in your handwriting.

(ELLIS looks at RONNIE. RONNIE looks back at him.)

ELLIS

Say it, then. Whatever it is. I'd rather have it than sit here while you go round it.

(FOZ takes a moment. She does not raise her voice.)

FOZ

You want me to say it? Huh?

(FOZ stops the tape recorder.)

FOZ

I don't think you came here because she was missing. I think you came here foolishly hoping that it was going to be enough for us to look the other way, and not see this for what it is.

ELLIS

WHAT?

FOZ

I think you met a nice, innocent woman, who was kind to you at the worst point of your life.

ELLIS

No.

FOZ

And then after six weeks she'd had enough of whatever this was. And she tells you as such.

But you, you just couldn't let go could you?

You couldn't have her leaving you be the last thing you made note of in that silly little book of yours. So you made sure it wasn't.

(ELLIS shakes his head hard. He doesn't speak.)

RONNIE

Ma'am.

(FOZ doesn't stop.)

FOZ

You wanted it to be the best thing that ever happened to you. And the only way that was possible was if YOU were the last thing that ever happened to her.

ELLIS

No. No, no. You monster. No.

(Silence.)

FOZ

So you gave her an ending. You closed the chapter for both of you while it was still good, and you took that woman's life.

And you wrote it up beautifully. So a year from now you look back, it is all rainbows and sunshines.

(A very long silence.)

FOZ

Ellis Croom, you are under arrest for the suspected murder of Nova. Ronnie, start the paperwork.

(ELLIS screams and cries, breaking down as FOZ walks away.)

(RONNIE looks at ELLIS as he looks up.)

ELLIS

Would I know?

(Beat.)

RONNIE

Would you know what?

ELLIS

If I'd done it. Would I still remember?

(ELLIS puts his hands over his face and goes down onto the table, and stays there.)

(Lights shift.)

PRESENT DAY.

(RONNIE back in the chair, plate on his knee. The kids, coats on.)

TOBES

So what happened? Did you find out if he actually did it?

RONNIE

Never found out. Last I heard he was doing life at Broadmoor. Not that it'd have been long.. given his condition and all...

TANYA

(with a sullen realisation)

Wow.. they didn't even know for sure if he did it...

(RONNIE nods.)

TOBES

And nobody even came looking for her?

RONNIE

Not as far as I could tell...

It's like the woman was a ghost. Six weeks and poof, she vanishes.

(A pause.)

TANYA

(quietly mouthing to herself but enough to reach Ronnie)

It's like Spirit Halloween.

(Beat.)

RONNIE

A what?

TOBES

Spirit Halloween. It's a retail shop. They show up during Halloween in October and on November 1st, they disappear without a trace.

TANYA

They take over a dead building unit for six weeks, everyone works on a temp contract, and gone by November 1st. No one knows where they come from, where they go. It's like their brand thing..

(A faint cry from downstairs.)

MOM

Tanya, Tobes. It's bedtime. Let grandpa rest.

(RONNIE sits at the desk in deep thought. He does not move.)

END TAG.

(The flashbacks run in his mind as a voiceover.)

"Retail. That's all I ever got."

"She had these hairpieces."

"She was late. That damned manager of hers."

"Tell me about the last day you saw her. Thirty-first of October."

"She didn't come."

"The blood is not real."

(Blackout.)
